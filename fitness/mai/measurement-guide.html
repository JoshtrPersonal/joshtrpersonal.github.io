Measurement guide

How To Measure Progress

Measure the same points, at the same time of day, in the same relaxed posture. The goal is consistency, not trying to force a smaller number.

The tape rule:

Start with the zero end of the tape on the front of the body, wrap the tape all the way around the same horizontal line, and read the number where the tape meets the zero mark again. Keep it snug enough to touch the skin or clothing, but not so tight that it dents in.

Tracking points
What To Measure

Body Weight Average

3–4 times/week Use the average

Measure from the scale, not from one emotional weigh-in.

  • Weigh in first thing in the morning, after the bathroom, before food or drink.
  • Stand still with feet centered on the scale.
  • Write down the number, then compare weekly averages instead of single days.

Natural Waist

Weekly Smallest point

Measure around the smallest point between the bottom ribs and the top of the hip bones.

  • Stand relaxed, feet about hip-width, stomach not sucked in.
  • Place the zero end at the front of the smallest waist point.
  • Wrap the tape around the back at the same height and read where it meets the zero end.

Belly Button Measurement

Weekly Lower stomach check

Measure straight around the body with the tape crossing the center of the belly button.

  • Keep the tape parallel to the floor from front to back.
  • Exhale normally, then measure without pulling the stomach in.
  • Use this number to track lower stomach change, bloating trends, and waistline consistency.

Hip / Glute Measurement

Every 2–4 weeks Widest point

Measure around the fullest part of the glutes, not the hip bones.

  • Stand with feet together or close together, weight even on both feet.
  • Wrap the tape around the widest point of the seat from front to back.
  • Check from the side that the tape is level and not sliding up at the back.

Front Progress Photo

Every 4 weeks Same setup

Measure visual progress by keeping the camera and body position repeatable.

  • Put the phone on a tripod or stable surface at about waist to chest height.
  • Use the same clothes, same room, same lighting, and same distance each time.
  • Stand relaxed with arms by sides, feet in the same place, and no twisting or posing.

Side / Back Progress Photos

Every 4 weeks Posture check

Use these to track stomach projection, posture, back line, and glute shape.

  • For the side photo, stand tall but normal: ribs down, shoulders relaxed, no sucking in.
  • For the back photo, face away from the camera with weight even on both feet.
  • Keep the same camera height and distance so changes are from the body, not the angle.
